Bookstack

Books I’ve read and what I took from them. Not reviews — notes to my future self, and a nudge if you’re looking for your next book.

Wings of Fire A.P.J. Abdul Kalam with Arun Tiwari

Read A.P.J. Abdul Kalam with Arun Tiwari

Wings of Fire

My first ever book. Kalam’s own story of growing up in Rameswaram and helping build India’s rocket programme. Read it for proof that patience, curiosity and a good teacher can carry you a very long way.

The Kalam Effect: My Years with the President P.M. Nair

Read P.M. Nair

The Kalam Effect: My Years with the President

Kalam’s secretary on five years inside Rashtrapati Bhavan. A good companion to Wings of Fire: it shows how the same values held up once he had real power.

The Emperor of All Maladies Siddhartha Mukherjee

Read Siddhartha Mukherjee

The Emperor of All Maladies

A biography of cancer — its history, the people who fought it, the ones who lost. Made me understand how science actually moves: not in clean leaps but in long, messy, human-sized struggles.

Invisible Cities Italo Calvino

Read Italo Calvino

Invisible Cities

Marco Polo describing cities to Kublai Khan — except none of the cities are real, and all of them are. Every city is some version of Venice, and every city is somewhere you’ve been in your mind.

Thinking, Fast and Slow Daniel Kahneman

Read Daniel Kahneman

Thinking, Fast and Slow

The book that made me distrust my instincts in the most useful way possible. Dense but worth the patience.

Pachinko Min Jin Lee

Read Min Jin Lee

Pachinko

Four generations of a Korean family in Japan. About identity, survival, shame, and what you inherit that you never asked for. The kind of novel that makes you want to call someone.

The Periodic Table Primo Levi

Reading now Primo Levi

The Periodic Table

Each chapter named for a chemical element, each one a story from Levi’s life as a chemist and Holocaust survivor. Quiet, precise, devastating. Reading slowly on purpose.

Surely You’re Joking, Mr. Feynman! Richard Feynman

Read Richard Feynman

Surely You’re Joking, Mr. Feynman!

Pure joy. A physicist who approached everything — physics, people, life — with the same irreverent curiosity. Made me want to be better at being interested in things.

Careless People Sarah Wynn-Williams

Read Sarah Wynn-Williams

Careless People

A former Facebook policy director on what she saw at the top of the company. Read it to understand how decisions that touch billions of people actually get made, and by whom.

Creativity, Inc. Ed Catmull with Amy Wallace

Read Ed Catmull with Amy Wallace

Creativity, Inc.

Pixar’s co-founder on keeping a creative team honest as it grows. Anyone who runs a team or a launch will find something to borrow, especially about giving and taking candid feedback.

Neelesh Misra ka Yaad Sheher Neelesh Misra

Read Neelesh Misra

Neelesh Misra ka Yaad Sheher

Stories from the imaginary small town Neelesh Misra built on the radio, now on paper. Read them for warm, unhurried Hindi storytelling, especially if you miss home.

Ruskin Bond’s stories Ruskin Bond

Read Ruskin Bond

Ruskin Bond’s stories

Short stories and essays from the hills around Mussoorie. Read him when you need to slow down; few writers make quiet days this absorbing.

I Came Upon a Lighthouse Shantanu Naidu

Read Shantanu Naidu

I Came Upon a Lighthouse

A young engineer’s memoir of his friendship with Ratan Tata, which began with a project to protect stray dogs. Read it for its warmth and for an everyday view of a very private man.

The Book of Clarity Paras Chopra

Read Paras Chopra

The Book of Clarity

The Wingify founder’s lessons on building companies, distilled. Read it before you start something; it covers the mistakes most founders only learn the hard way.

28 Jobs, 28 Weeks, 28 States Jubanashwa Mishra

Read Jubanashwa Mishra

28 Jobs, 28 Weeks, 28 States

At 28, an engineer leaves the expected path and works a different job each week in a different state. Read it for a ground-level view of how India works, and for the nerve it takes to try.

Poor Economics Abhijit V. Banerjee and Esther Duflo

Read Abhijit V. Banerjee and Esther Duflo

Poor Economics

Two Nobel-winning economists on how people living in poverty actually make decisions. Read it to swap assumptions for evidence.

Why I Am an Atheist Bhagat Singh

Read Bhagat Singh

Why I Am an Atheist

An essay Bhagat Singh wrote in jail in 1930, at 23. Read it, whatever you believe, for how carefully he reasons and how calmly he faces the question.

Shoe Dog Phil Knight

Read Phil Knight

Shoe Dog

Nike’s founder on the near-misses of its early years. Read it for an honest picture of how messy building a company really is.

R.N. Kao: Gentleman Spymaster Nitin A. Gokhale

Read Nitin A. Gokhale

R.N. Kao: Gentleman Spymaster

The life of the man who set up R&AW, India’s external intelligence agency. Read it for a side of Indian history that is rarely told.

Why I Supported the Emergency Khushwant Singh, edited by Sheela Reddy

Read Khushwant Singh, edited by Sheela Reddy

Why I Supported the Emergency

Decades of Khushwant Singh’s essays and profiles, including the title essay defending his most unpopular stand. Read it for a sharp, funny, unfiltered voice you will often argue with.

The Palace of Illusions Chitra Banerjee Divakaruni

Read Chitra Banerjee Divakaruni

The Palace of Illusions

The Mahabharata retold by Draupadi. Read it to meet a story you thought you knew through the woman at its centre.

Steve Jobs Walter Isaacson

Read Walter Isaacson

Steve Jobs

The authorised biography, built on long interviews with Jobs and the people around him. Read it to see how taste, products and temper came together, flaws included.

Six Easy Pieces Richard P. Feynman

Read Richard P. Feynman

Six Easy Pieces

The six most approachable chapters from Feynman’s physics lectures. Read it to understand atoms, energy and gravity from someone who loved explaining them.

Zero to One Peter Thiel with Blake Masters

Read Peter Thiel with Blake Masters

Zero to One

Notes on startups built around one idea: create something new instead of copying what already works. Read it to sharpen how you think about building.
